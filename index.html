About Research Teaching CV

I am an incoming Postdoctoral Fellow in Cities and Communities at the Johns Hopkins University School of Government and Policy. I am also a Research Affiliate of the Political Economy Lab at MIT and a Good Life Fellow at the Roosevelt Institute.

My research examines how large-scale transformations in finance and governance reshape housing, land use, and urban development in the United States. Working at the intersection of urban planning, geography, and political economy, I investigate the mechanisms through which housing and land are transformed into financial assets, the consequences of these transformations for residents and communities, and the forms of collective action that emerge in response. Through community-engaged and mixed-methods research, I explore how people navigate, contest, and reimagine the institutions that govern urban life. My work spans rental housing and housing finance, land use and redevelopment, digital infrastructures, and community planning, with particular attention to local context and the histories of racialization that shape access to urban space.

I hold a Ph.D. and Master’s in City Planning from MIT and a B.A. in Philosophy and Biology from the University of Chicago. Before graduate school, I worked as a public health researcher and patient educator at the University of Chicago.
